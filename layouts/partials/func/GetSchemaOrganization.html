The other places only lend their address, listed after the head office one: Google reads several on an organization present in more than one location. A single one stays a single object. The coordinates are the head office ones, and only an organization that is a place too — a LocalBusiness, see func/GetSchemaTypes.html — takes them. The head office is the organization itself: passed here as an office, it comes back as the organization, not as an office of itself. Passing a place turns the node into that office: it keeps the logo of the whole firm, overrides whatever the place describes for itself, takes its own @id and points back at the organization through parentOrganization. That is how a person attached to a place ends up working for their office rather than for the firm as a whole. The social profiles, the names, the distinctions and the identifiers stay the firm's: an office only lists its own. A place only qualifies as an office when its schema.type names an organization subtype — the places section is generic, and a venue or a landmark is a location, not an organization. Otherwise it stays a place: see func/GetSchemaPlace.html, and func/GetSchemaEntity.html which picks between the two. The key is nested rather than a top level `type`, which Hugo reserves in front matter to select the content type. The page listing the offices, the contact page, makes an office of every place of the organization, its head office included, see func/GetSchemaOffices.html. A place kept in data has no page of its own: it takes the url of that page and an anchor of it as its @id. An office is named after the firm, unless its title names it already. Params: {map} optional compact (bool) return only what another node needs to reference this one: @type, @id, name, url and the logo — which Google reads on a publisher or a hiringOrganization.
